Whether you are new to PULSE or extending something you have already built, this page lays out the
development routes available to you, and the account and tooling each one needs.

<Callout title="Who builds this">
  PULSE is made by **Resolve Labs**, a small team working on one question: which product
  information is worth paying to fix. The marketing site, which covers the why rather than the
  how, is <Ext to="site">thepulseapi.com</Ext>. This site is for the people who have to wire it
  up.
</Callout>

## The advantage of starting at the catalogue

Most tools in this space rank what a shopper sees. PULSE asks the question underneath that one:
can your catalogue answer what they came to find out at all?

A shopper leaves without buying for one of two reasons. Either they could not find the product,
or your catalogue could not tell two products apart. The second is larger and almost nobody
measures it. On a 216-product laptop shelf we measured, **94 products remain indistinguishable**
after every question a shopper could answer, because the attributes that would separate them are
not recorded anywhere. No ranking model fixes that. Only recording the attribute does.

So your own product data is the foundation, and everything here is a route to working with it:

- **Measuring a catalogue** with [the audit endpoint](/api/v1/audit). It takes a category export
  and returns the attributes worth recording, ranked by how many shoppers need them rather than
  by how empty the column is. Stateless, and it stores nothing unless you ask it to.
- **Proving a repair worked** with [the verify endpoint](/api/v1/audit), which runs the same
  measurement on the repaired export and reports what moved, attribute by attribute. It reads
  your catalogue rather than your traffic, so there is nothing to wait for.
- **Reading live sessions** with [the browser SDK](/sdk), which sends signals and gets back a
  ranking of your catalogue against what the session implies, every score with a per-factor
  breakdown. This is the only part that touches your storefront, and it is optional.
- **Integrating where you already work**, through [framework guides](/frameworks),
  [storefront platforms](/platforms) and [server libraries](/backend) for eight languages.
- **Letting an agent read these docs** through [the MCP server](/mcp), so an assistant answers
  from the documentation itself rather than from memory.

Nothing goes on your site to begin with. The first useful answer comes from a file.

| The question | What PULSE answers with |
| --- | --- |
| **Which missing field costs the most?** | A ranking by how many shoppers need it, not by how empty the column is |
| **Where does that fact already live?** | Often in your own titles and descriptions, recoverable before anyone types anything |
| **Did the repair work?** | The same measurement on the repaired export, with no traffic needed |

## What you need before the first request

Create an account at <Ext to="signUp">the dashboard</Ext>, then copy a key from
<Ext to="apiKey">the API settings screen</Ext>. Every request on this site needs one. The
versioned API root every example posts to is <Url to="api" />.

The audit needs one more thing and nothing else: a single category of products as JSON. No
traffic, no tag on your site, no history.

## What does the whole journey look like?

Six steps, in the order you will meet them. Each one is useful on its own, and you can stop
after any of them.

<Steps>

<Step>

### Measure the catalogue

`POST /catalog/audit` takes a category export and returns the attributes worth recording, ranked,
with the affected products attached. Stateless, and it stores nothing unless you ask it to.

[Run your first audit](/quickstart)

</Step>

<Step>

### Understand what came back

The ranking is measured in bits of decision information rather than guessed from clicks, and the
emptiest column is usually the wrong one to fix. This explains what that means and, more
usefully, the three cases where it is wrong.

[What the audit measures](/concepts/what-the-audit-measures)

</Step>

<Step>

### Repair, then verify

Your team populates the top attribute. `POST /catalog/verify` runs the same measurement on the
repaired export and reports what moved, attribute by attribute. It reads your catalogue rather
than your traffic, so there is nothing to wait for.

[Verify endpoint](/api/v1/audit)

</Step>

<Step>

### Put it in your stack

Only needed once you want the live parts. Framework guides for React, Vue, Angular, Astro, Alpine
and Laravel; storefront guides for Shopify, WordPress and Magento; server guides for eight
languages.

[Framework guides](/frameworks) · [Storefronts](/platforms) · [Backend](/backend)

</Step>

<Step>

### Rank live sessions, if you want to

The optional part, and the only part that touches your storefront. The SDK sends signals, the
engine ranks your catalogue against what the session implies, and every score comes with a
per-factor breakdown.

[SDK reference](/sdk) · [API reference](/api/v1)

</Step>

<Step>

### Go live

What to check before you ship, what to watch afterwards, and the things we will not claim.

[Going live](/going-live)

</Step>

</Steps>

## Install

<InstallTabs pkg="@thepulseapi/sdk" />

Or drop it on the page, which is what the storefront integrations use:

```html title="index.html"
<script src="https://cdn.thepulseapi.com/sdk/v0/pulse.iife.js" async></script>
```

The script tag exposes the client as the global `Pulse`, so `Pulse.init(...)` works directly.

<Callout title="What leaves the browser">
  Signals and a session id. Not names, not emails, not payment data. The audit never sees a
  shopper at all: it reads product data.
</Callout>
