Most tools here rank what a shopper sees. PULSE asks the question underneath that one: can your
catalogue answer what they came to find out at all? You send a category export, get back the
attributes worth recording in priority order, and re-measure once your team has filled them in.

Nothing goes on your site to begin with. The first useful answer comes from a file.

<Callout title="Who builds this">
  PULSE is made by **Resolve Labs**, a small team working on one question: which product
  information is worth paying to fix. The marketing site is
  [thepulseapi.com](https://thepulseapi.com/en). This site is for the people who have to wire it
  up.
</Callout>

## What problem does this actually solve?

A shopper leaves without buying. Either they could not find the product, or your catalogue could
not tell them apart.

The second one is larger and almost nobody measures it. On a 216-product laptop shelf we
measured, **94 products remain indistinguishable** after every question a shopper could answer,
because the attributes that would separate them are not recorded anywhere. No ranking model fixes
that. Only recording the attribute does.

So PULSE starts at the catalogue rather than at the session:

| The question | What PULSE answers with |
| --- | --- |
| **Which missing field costs the most?** | A ranking by how many shoppers need it, not by how empty the column is |
| **Where does that fact already live?** | Often in your own titles and descriptions, recoverable before anyone types anything |
| **Did the repair work?** | The same measurement on the repaired export, with no traffic needed |

## What does the whole journey look like?

Six steps, in the order you will meet them. Each one is useful on its own, and you can stop
after any of them.

<Steps>

<Step>

### Measure the catalogue

`POST /catalog/audit` takes a category export and returns the attributes worth recording, ranked,
with the affected products attached. Stateless, and it stores nothing unless you ask it to.

[Run your first audit](/quickstart)

</Step>

<Step>

### Understand what came back

The ranking is measured in bits of decision information rather than guessed from clicks, and the
emptiest column is usually the wrong one to fix. This explains what that means and, more
usefully, the three cases where it is wrong.

[What the audit measures](/concepts/what-the-audit-measures)

</Step>

<Step>

### Repair, then verify

Your team populates the top attribute. `POST /catalog/verify` runs the same measurement on the
repaired export and reports what moved, attribute by attribute. It reads your catalogue rather
than your traffic, so there is nothing to wait for.

[Verify endpoint](/api/v1/audit)

</Step>

<Step>

### Put it in your stack

Only needed once you want the live parts. Framework guides for React, Vue, Angular, Astro, Alpine
and Laravel; storefront guides for Shopify, WordPress and Magento; server guides for eight
languages.

[Framework guides](/frameworks) · [Storefronts](/platforms) · [Backend](/backend)

</Step>

<Step>

### Rank live sessions, if you want to

The optional part, and the only part that touches your storefront. The SDK sends signals, the
engine ranks your catalogue against what the session implies, and every score comes with a
per-factor breakdown.

[SDK reference](/sdk) · [API reference](/api/v1)

</Step>

<Step>

### Go live

What to check before you ship, what to watch afterwards, and the things we will not claim.

</Step>

</Steps>

## Install

<InstallTabs pkg="@thepulseapi/sdk" />

Or drop it on the page, which is what the storefront integrations use:

```html title="index.html"
<script src="https://cdn.thepulseapi.com/sdk/v0/pulse.iife.js" async></script>
```

The script tag exposes the client as the global `Pulse`, so `Pulse.init(...)` works directly.

<Callout title="What leaves the browser">
  Signals and a session id. Not names, not emails, not payment data. The audit never sees a
  shopper at all: it reads product data.
</Callout>
