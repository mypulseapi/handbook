Two different things carry two different licences, and the distinction matters before you build
on either.

**PULSE is proprietary software.** The measurement — how decision information is computed, how
attributes are ranked, how a session is read — is not open source and is not published. We explain
what it does and why, in detail, throughout this documentation. We do not publish how it does it.

The SDK is genuinely MIT and genuinely open. Read every line before you ship it; that is the point
of publishing it. It is a thin client: it can send signals and read a ranking, and it can do
nothing else. **Nothing in the SDK reveals or reproduces the engine**, so forking it gets you a
client, not an implementation.

<Callout type="warn" title="What this means in practice">
  You may use the API under your agreement, and you may read, modify and redistribute the SDK
  under MIT. You may not reverse engineer the engine, resell access to it, or use its outputs to
  train a competing model. Those are terms of the agreement rather than a licence header, and they
  are the ones people actually ask about.
</Callout>

## What counts against your quota

Every organisation has a monthly call allowance set by its plan. Not every endpoint spends it.

| Counts | Does not count |
| --- | --- |
| `/track` and `/track/**` | `/suggest` |
| `/search`, `/rerank` | `/event` and `/event/**` |
| `/catalog/**`, including the audit and verify | `/exposure` and `/exposure/**` |
| `/session` and `/session/**` | |

The split is deliberate: **telling us what happened is free, asking us to decide something is
not.** `/event` and `/exposure` are how you report back what a shopper actually saw and did, and
charging for that would discourage exactly the data that makes the next answer better.

The gateway returns your remaining allowance on every counted call, so you never have to guess
where you are. Exceeding it is a refusal, not a surprise invoice.

## Rate limits

Separate from the quota, and they apply per route rather than per plan. The quota is monthly
volume; these stop bursts.

Sensitive public routes are limited tightly, because they are the ones worth attacking:

| Route | Limit |
| --- | --- |
| `/auth/forgot-password` | 3 per hour |
| Public contact form | 3 per day |
| Public newsletter signup | 1 per 30 days |
| Public careers application | 5 per 30 days |

Per-IP limits apply on top, and the limit follows the visitor rather than your server: the gateway
reads the forwarded chain, so one shopper hitting a limit does not exhaust it for everyone behind
your storefront.

A limited request answers `429` with a `retryAfter`. **Back off and retry; do not spin.** A client
that retries immediately on 429 is indistinguishable from the abuse these limits exist to stop,
and will be treated as such.

## Authentication, and which key goes where

Three kinds of credential, and using the wrong one is the most common integration failure:

- **API key** (`apikey`) — the runtime surface: track, search, rerank, catalog, session. This is
  the publishable key the browser SDK carries. It can send signals and read rankings and nothing
  else, which is why it is safe in a page.
- **JWT** (`jwt`) — everything a merchant does as themselves: products, categories, uploads,
  billing, team. **Never put one in a browser bundle.**
- **Public** — sign-in, registration, password reset. Rate limited as above.

Key management lives in <Ext to="apiKey">the dashboard</Ext>. Rotate a key the moment you think it
has leaked; it takes effect immediately and old keys stop working.

## Security and abuse

We monitor for the ordinary things: credential stuffing against the auth routes, scraping patterns
against the catalogue endpoints, and keys used from places they have never been used before.

**What gets an account suspended:** bulk extraction of ranking outputs, using one key across
unrelated catalogues, or any attempt to reconstruct the engine's behaviour from its responses.
Those are rare, and they are also obvious from the call pattern.

**What does not:** a burst during a sale, a misconfigured retry you fix, a load test you tell us
about first. Tell us before the load test and nothing happens; run it unannounced and the limits
will treat it as what it looks like.

<Callout title="Found a vulnerability?">
  Mail <Ext to="support">support@thepulseapi.com</Ext> with enough detail to reproduce it, and give
  us a chance to fix it before publishing. We will not pursue anyone who reports in good faith and
  does not exfiltrate data or degrade the service while proving the point.
</Callout>

## What we commit to

The API is versioned, and `/api/v1` keeps its shape. [Versioning](/api/versioning) covers what a
breaking change means here and how long you get.

The audit stores nothing unless you ask it to. It reads product data and never sees a shopper:
no customer records, no order history, no personal data. That is a property of the measurement
rather than a policy, which is why it is in [how it works](/concepts/how-it-works) as well as here.
