**This documentation is for approved PULSE merchants.** The API is not open, there is no free
public tier, and an API key is issued only to an organisation that has registered, subscribed and
been approved.

If that is not you yet, the next section is the whole of what you need. Everything after it
assumes you are already in.

## You need an approved account

Three steps, in this order. There is no way to skip one.

<Steps>

<Step>

### Register your organisation

Create an account at <Ext to="signUp">the dashboard</Ext> and verify your email address. You are
registering an **organisation**, not a personal login: the catalogue, the keys and the quota all
belong to it.

</Step>

<Step>

### Subscribe

Choose a plan. The plan sets your monthly call allowance, which
[Licence, limits and fair use](/licence-and-limits) explains in full. Pricing is on
<Ext to="site">thepulseapi.com</Ext>.

</Step>

<Step>

### Wait for approval

Registration is reviewed by our team, normally **within one to two business days**. You will get
an email when the decision is made.

Until it lands, your account exists but your keys do not work. That is deliberate rather than a
queue: we look at who is asking before handing out access to the engine.

</Step>

</Steps>

<Callout type="warn" title="Not approved yet?">
  There is nothing in this documentation you can run. The endpoints will refuse an unapproved
  key, and no amount of correct code gets past that.

  Subscribe at <Ext to="signUp">the dashboard</Ext>, or write to <Ext to="sales" /> if you want a
  first Decision Audit before committing to a plan. Send one category export and we will send the
  findings back.
</Callout>

<Callout title="Why it works this way">
  The engine is proprietary and the measurement is not published, so access is granted to a known
  organisation under an agreement rather than to anyone with an email address. It also lets us
  apply rate limits and abuse controls to an account that has a person behind it.
  [Licence, limits and fair use](/licence-and-limits) covers the rest.
</Callout>

### New to PULSE, learning the product

**Start with [how it works](/concepts/how-it-works), not with the API.** Nothing goes on your site
to get the first answer, and there is no reason to integrate before you have seen what the
measurement says about your own products.

What you need: one category exported as JSON. Not your whole catalogue, and not a clean one. The
audit is most useful on the category you are least sure about.

1. [How it works](/concepts/how-it-works), so the number means something when you see it.
2. [What the audit measures](/concepts/what-the-audit-measures) — why the emptiest column is
   usually the wrong one to fix, and the three cases where the ranking is wrong.
3. [The quickstart](/quickstart), when you want to run one.

You do not need a developer for any of that. A file in, a ranked list back.

### You run an ecommerce store

You have the catalogue, so the question is getting it out of your platform and acting on what
comes back.

**Go to [the quickstart](/quickstart).** About ten lines, and the export usually takes under an
hour out of a PIM or a storefront admin. Then
[what the audit measures](/concepts/what-the-audit-measures), so the ranked list is actionable
rather than interesting.

<Callout type="warn" title="There is no plugin yet">
  No Shopify app, no WooCommerce plugin, no Magento extension. Integration today is the API and
  the browser SDK, and the audit needs neither. Anyone describing a thirty minute plugin install
  is describing something we have not built.
</Callout>

After the repairs, [the verify endpoint](/api/v1/audit) runs the same measurement on the repaired
export and reports what moved, attribute by attribute. It reads your catalogue rather than your
traffic, so there is nothing to wait for.

### You have developers

Hand them [the API reference](/api/v1) and [the SDK](/sdk). Both assume you can read an HTTP
request and nothing else about our domain.

1. [Authentication and the versioned root](/api/v1) — every request needs an approved key.
2. [The audit endpoint](/api/v1/audit) — stateless, stores nothing unless asked.
3. [Framework guides](/frameworks) or [server libraries](/backend) for wherever the call lives.
4. [Going live](/going-live) — what to check before shipping, and what we will not claim.

The live session layer is optional and is the only part touching a storefront. A merchant who
only wants the audit installs nothing.

<Callout title="Point your assistant at this site">
  If your developers use Claude, Cursor or another MCP client, [the MCP server](/mcp) lets it
  answer from this documentation rather than from memory. Usually faster than reading, and it
  cannot invent an endpoint that does not exist.
</Callout>

## What the audit needs from your industry

The same two things everywhere: a category export, and products a shopper actually has to choose
between. Below is what the deciding attribute tends to be in each, because the first question
everyone asks is whether this applies to what they sell.

| Industry | What shoppers decide on | What is usually missing |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Ecommerce and retail** | Whatever separates two near-identical products on a deep shelf | The field nobody recorded, because the catalogue grew by adding products rather than columns |
| **FMCG and consumer goods** | Pack size, dietary claim, concentration, whether it is a refill | Facts printed on the label and never entered as data |
| **Technology and electronics** | Specifications, down to a single number | The spec separating two devices one number apart |
| **Furniture and homewares** | Dimensions and materials; whether it fits | Measurements, when the listing sells on the photograph |
| **Cosmetics and personal care** | Skin type, ingredients, the concern being treated | Suitability, which was never a field |
| **B2B and industrial** | Specification: thread size, voltage, tolerance | Specs in a supplier PDF nobody mapped, in a catalogue built for order codes |

**If your industry is not listed, the audit still runs.** It reads whatever attributes your export
contains and ranks them. The list above is where we have measured, not a supported-industry list.

**What we can help with.** Reading your audit output with you, telling you which of the three
failure cases you are in, and saying when the ranking is wrong for your catalogue. We will look at
a real export; it is the only way to answer most questions properly.

**What we will not do.** Quote a conversion uplift. We have run no live revenue experiment, so we
have no number, and any percentage attributed to us did not come from us.
[Going live](/going-live) has the rest of what we will not claim.

Replies usually come the same working day, from the people who do the work rather than from a
queue.
