Most of what stops a shopper buying is a fact your catalogue does not record: the screen size, the
fit, whether it works with what they already own. These four products find those gaps and prove
the repairs, from a catalogue export and your order history. **No storefront change, no tracking
script, no traffic needed.**

| Product | The question it answers | Call | Counts toward usage |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| [Decision Audit](/catalogue/decision-audit) | Which missing facts cost the most buying decisions, and which do I fix first? | `POST /catalog/audit` | Yes |
| [Decision Verification](/catalogue/decision-verification) | Did the repair actually make the catalogue answer what shoppers ask? | `POST /catalog/verify` with `repairs` | Yes |
| [Decision Monitor](/catalogue/decision-monitor) | What has slipped since the last export, and where? | `POST /catalog/verify` without `repairs` | Yes |
| [Return Verification](/catalogue/return-verification) | Which missing facts go with returns, and did a repair move them? | `POST /outcomes` | **No** |

## The order they are used in

<Steps>
<Step>
### Audit one category

Start with the shelf that matters most. The audit returns the attributes worth recording, ranked
by how many buying decisions each one moves.
</Step>
<Step>
### Repair, then verify

Fill the top of the list, export again, and send both exports. Verification grades each claimed
fix against what actually changed.
</Step>
<Step>
### Keep it measured

Run the same comparison on every new export. A catalogue decays as suppliers change feeds and new
products arrive, and the monitor says where.
</Step>
<Step>
### Check the outcome that costs money

Report what happened to each order line and Return Verification shows which gaps go with returns,
and whether the repairs moved them.
</Step>
</Steps>

Ready to show it to shoppers too? The [storefront products](/storefront) rank a live visit against
the catalogue you just repaired.
