Most search and recommendation tools answer, because answering is all they can do. They rank
something for any query, including the queries your catalogue has no data to resolve.

PULSE does two things those do not.

<Cards>
  <Card icon={<Activity />} title="Infers in-session" description="Persona accumulates from behaviour within a single visit, with no identity and no history." />
  <Card icon={<SearchX />} title="Knows when it cannot answer" description="If the catalogue cannot resolve a requirement, that is a reportable fact rather than a bad ranking." />
</Cards>

## The loop

<Steps>
<Step>
### A signal arrives
A category view, a price filter, a device class. See [Signals](/concepts/signals).
</Step>
<Step>
### The persona updates
Each signal moves a set of persona scores. Scores decay, so a session that changes direction
is followed rather than averaged.
</Step>
<Step>
### Products are scored against it
`search` ranks products you pass in. `rankedCatalog` ranks the catalogue already synced to PULSE.
</Step>
<Step>
### Exposure is recorded
What was actually shown is what makes ranking quality measurable later. See
[`exposure`](/api/v1/exposure).
</Step>
</Steps>

## What it is not

<Callout type="warn">
  PULSE is not a PIM and it replaces nothing you already run. It reads your catalogue and ranks
  against it. If a field is missing everywhere, ranking cannot invent it, and saying so is the
  point.
</Callout>
